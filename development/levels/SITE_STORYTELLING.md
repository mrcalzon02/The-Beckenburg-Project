# Site storytelling: Blackpine first

The location needs a past that can be inferred before reading a document. Detail is placed for a reason: work, comfort, maintenance, weather, departure or later occupation. Empty working space and clear routes remain part of the composition. This is a reusable first kit, not a claim that a thousand distinct objects or a finished location have been delivered.

## Authored Blackpine chronology

- **November 2009:** logging work suspended. The foreman expected a fortnight's interruption. Coats, beds, tools and family belongings remained. The final payroll records eleven signatures; dispatch lists nine crew places on the last truck. This unresolved administrative discrepancy does not establish anyone's death or identify the ghost.
- **The empty years:** water isolated, oil plant out of service, window-frame leaks, damp walls, surface splits, worn thresholds and buckets beneath drips. These marks do not remove the physical walking floor.
- **February 2025:** two survivors displaced by Emergence Day shelter for three nights in the logging cabin. One blue bedroll, clothes, opened tins, a bottle, wrappers and a journal form a small localized cluster. The west window is boarded. Their note leaves the old office records untouched.
- **March 2025:** a caretaker records a Meridian road inspection and isolates generator fuel and records the locked inspection power inlet, the dry water supply and the moved washhouse bucket. Existing gameplay lighting circuits still operate through maintenance power.

These are fictional authored dates, not dates calculated from the player's clock. Weathering and fresh traces should remain consistent with this layering. Campaign links to other locations remain provisional records to investigate.

## Reusable kit

[Asset manifest](story-detail-assets.json) records native bounds, triangles, hashes, license and editable sources. **Nineteen new original CC0 meshes, 5,255 source triangles:** open records with pens/pencil/papers, journal, calendar, hooked coat, framed photograph, clothing pile, bedroll, opened tins/wrappers/bottle, timber wear, exposed-lath wall damage, damp streaks, leak bucket/stain, nailed window boards, woodstove/flue/hearth, fused electrical branch/conduit/outlet, generator/inlet, oil water heater/feed/flue, wash trough/drain/taps, and camp bed/bedding. Existing worn work boots, table, counter, bench and fireplace meshes are reused.

Each GLB preserves its metre pivot, UVs and procedural diffuse images, with a matching editable Blender source and studio render. Wall modules face local +Z. Damage and paper are shallow surface geometry; bucket, bedding, furniture, heaters, flues and boarding have physical obstruction appropriate to their shape. Boards block the two real window apertures; the frames can still be operated behind the boarding. Clothing, papers and rubbish are authored static scenery, not new pickup or simulation systems.

Nine actual readable records use the existing centre-ray E interaction and reading panel. Twelve room-history metadata sockets record use/heating/condition/occupation and participate in the map revision. The kit uses the shared scenery renderer. It adds no menu or HUD redesign.

## Apply to later maps and backport deliberately

Before dressing each location, write its occupation/closure/reuse timeline and a room ledger covering purpose, heat source, power source, water/drainage, maintenance, possessions, departure and later visitors. Give repeated props local meaning: boots beside warmth, oil near service equipment, wear between destinations, damp below an actual vulnerable opening, and food beside a sleeping area. Contrast old dust and faded belongings with a small, coherent newer cluster. Do not scatter every prop into every room.

Support objects on real surfaces; connect pipes/flues to their destination; keep portals, spawn points, walking aisles, stairs and ritual footprints clear. Collision, terrain, rendering and readable aiming must agree. Reuse this kit across the atlas once each map has its own authored story; earlier maps have not been automatically redressed by this change.

Acceptance includes source/hash/texture checks, physical support and centre-ray reading, full swept navigation, ritual clearance, actual renderer views and native scene inspection. Remaining Blackpine work includes working-mill machinery, site signage, a finer weather/lighting pass, hunt/ritual acceptance and production integration. Current history is not paranormal evidence or a completed campaign.
